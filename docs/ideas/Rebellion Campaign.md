# Rebellion - as a strategic layer

An overarching campaign layer: a rebel faction operates from a hidden base, taking territory from a tyrannical count until the heroes can challenge and defeat him.

- Keep the game centred on the heroes and their existing missions and fights.
- Add drama through resource management, military commitments and persistent territory, beyond choosing a road.
- Manage rebel forces collectively; no individual soldier management.
- This is an idea under exploration, not a replacement for the current run design yet.

## Campaign map

- A node-based country with local garrisons, forts, holdings and the count's castle.
- Holdings provide resources for the campaign; resources build up rebel troops and unlock power-ups for heroes.
- Holding lords can serve as bosses. Defeating them offers substantial benefits and provokes a stronger response.
- Conquest raises two questions: can we take it, and can we hold it?

> [!frontier] Resource economy
> Troops, supplies and gold were suggested. Exact resources, income, upkeep and hero upgrade costs remain open.

## Army operations and hero missions

- Rebel armies contest forts against the count's forces. Strategic fights unfold over several campaign turns according to the relative forces; both sides can reinforce them.
- During an operation, the heroes undertake missions such as disrupting enemy reinforcements to tip the outcome in the rebellion's favour.
- Hero missions largely retain the existing gameplay.
- The final victory requires the heroes to challenge and defeat the count in combat. The rebel army cannot replace that confrontation or win the campaign by overpowering him.
- The desired boss encounter is a personal challenge against the ruler; army warfare should not become the heroes fighting the count and his army.

> [!frontier] Campaign time
> What advances a campaign turn, and how many opportunities can the heroes act on while an army operation unfolds?

## The count's response

- The count begins confident that his rule is secure and dismisses early skirmishes as rabble.
- As the rebellion grows, he commits detachments to hunt it, escalating until he sends all his armies at the final threshold.
- His forces are disproportionate from the start, but he does not know the hidden base's location. Missions can influence progress towards its discovery.
- Discovery provokes a massive assault and threatens campaign defeat.

> [!frontier] Discovery crisis
> An announced crisis with an opportunity to respond was welcomed. Evacuation, diversion and desperate defence are possible responses; exact rules remain open.

> [!frontier] Escalation and discovery
> Separate pressure tracks were suggested. Their triggers, relationship and visibility remain undecided.

## Redistributing enemy power

> [!frontier] Node levels and troop movements
> Promising direction: node levels represent power. The count's node is initially impossible to attempt; rebel expansion draws forces out across the map, gradually opening paths for the heroes. Actual troop movements could make these changes readable. How this opens a personal boss challenge without tying its difficulty to army strength remains unresolved.

## Hero and army progression

> [!fog] Two readiness requirements
> Preparing the army to hold a conquest may overlevel the heroes for its boss. Separating military readiness from hero readiness still leaves two progression requirements competing over the same objective. The relationship between these systems is the central unresolved problem.

> [!frontier] Military commitments instead of a parallel power ladder
> Latest suggestion, not agreed: troops and supplies determine the costs, risks and sacrifices involved in supporting a hero objective, rather than imposing another strength threshold. A weaker rebellion might expose or abandon another holding to support an attempt. Whether this preserves the intended campaign management needs deciding.

> [!frontier] Campaign length and presentation
> Campaign duration and the relationship to the existing act structure remain open. The rebellion story could retain the puppet theatre presentation; no presentation change has been decided.
